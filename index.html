Recovery from records estates would otherwise abandon.

I help insolvency trustees and receivers turn a failed technology company's internal records into recovery for creditors, without creating exposure for the estate.

The problem

When a technology company fails, its internal records are rarely scheduled as an asset. They're spread across code repositories, ticketing systems, shared drives and chat tools. Nobody on the estate has time to review them, and disposing of them carelessly can create privacy and liability exposure. So the rational default is to abandon them.

What changed

AI developers now license real engineering and operational records — the material that shows how work actually gets done inside a company. That gives records an estate would otherwise abandon real value to creditors.

What I do

  1. InventoryWork out what exists: repositories, documentation, project history, internal communications.
  2. ScreenConfirm the estate owns it and can convey it, flag anything held in licensed third-party systems, and identify personal information for removal or exclusion under PIPEDA and provincial privacy law.
  3. Package and marketPrepare the records for sale and take them to qualified buyers.
  4. Structure the transferWork with the estate's counsel on sale terms so the estate isn't exposed.

How I work

I act for the seller, not the buyer. I don't purchase records.

What I won't touch

Health or patient records, student records, consumer or children's personal data, and anything under litigation hold.
